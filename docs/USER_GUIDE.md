# AML-Sim User Guide

## A laboratory for synthetic financial markets

AML-Sim is a multi-agent financial simulation platform. It creates a synthetic
market in which different market participants place orders, trade with one
another, respond to news and stress, and adapt their behavior as the market
changes.

The aim is not to predict tomorrow's price or replay a historical trading day.
Instead, AML-Sim lets you change one part of a market, repeat the simulation,
and see how that change affects liquidity, prices, risk, and participant
behavior.

## What you can explore

AML-Sim can be used to study questions such as:

- How does firm-specific news affect the market where it originates?
- How does a funding or interest-rate shock affect several connected markets?
- Does information move between a stock and its future before trading links do?
- Which participant roles provide or withdraw liquidity during stress?
- How do inventory limits and risk appetite change market-maker behavior?
- Do adaptive strategies recover after shocks or amplify instability?
- Does a strategy that looks robust in one market remain robust when markets
  are connected?
- How do deterministic strategies differ from LLM-assisted strategies under
  the same conditions?

Because the scenario and random seed are recorded, the same experiment can be
repeated and compared with a controlled variation.

## How the simulated market works

Every simulation contains one or more exchanges. Each exchange has an order
book where participants submit bids, asks, market orders, and cancellations.
Orders are matched using StockSim's exchange engine, and completed trades create
the synthetic price path.

Prices are therefore generated by the interaction of the participants. AAPL,
AAPL_FUT, and similar names in the included scenarios are experimental
instrument labels; their simulated paths are not live prices or forecasts for
the real securities.

Current scenarios demonstrate:

- a single synthetic stock market;
- connected stock and futures markets;
- connected stock, futures, and bond markets;
- multi-level order books and trade queues;
- participant-level cash, positions, exposure, and profit and loss;
- micro and macro shocks;
- information and arbitrage links between markets.

The architecture is intended to extend to options and ETFs, but specialist
pricing, hedging, and settlement behavior for every asset class is not yet part
of the completed simulation model.

## Market participants

AML-Sim includes several participant roles. Each role has a different objective
and a different way of turning market information into orders.

### Market maker

Posts bids and asks at several price levels and manages its inventory. It can
widen its spread, reduce quote size, or withdraw liquidity when volatility and
risk increase.

### Retail participant

Places smaller and less frequent orders. Its behavior can reflect sentiment,
recent price momentum, herding, panic, and sensitivity to news.

### Institutional participant

Executes larger objectives through smaller child orders. It can use target
execution, momentum, or mean-reversion logic while respecting position and risk
limits.

### Informed participant

Trades when the market price differs sufficiently from its private estimate of
fundamental value. Its information edge and willingness to act are configurable.

### Liquidity taker

Consumes resting orders more aggressively and represents directional demand or
supply pressure.

### Cross-market arbitrageur

Observes an explicitly defined relationship, such as a stock and its future,
and attempts to trade price differences across the two order books.

### Shock agent

Does not trade. It introduces news, policy changes, funding stress, liquidity
events, and other uncertainty into the simulation. It also controls who learns
about an event and when.

## How agents make decisions

Trading agents have two connected decision loops.

### Fast action loop

The fast loop runs frequently and performs the role's immediate trading task.
For example, a market maker refreshes quotes while a retail participant decides
whether to submit a small order. This loop is deterministic code operating
within the current strategy and risk limits.

### Slow strategy loop

The slow loop runs less frequently and reviews a broader context: recent market
prices, the order book, inventory, cash, fills, known events, active shocks,
memory, and the current strategy. It may change bounded strategy parameters for
the next sequence of fast-loop actions.

Two slow-loop modes are available:

- **Frozen:** keeps the strategy controlled and deterministic. This is useful
  for repeatable baselines and causal comparisons.
- **OpenAI:** asks an LLM to review the context and propose a strategy update.
  The proposal must pass validation before it can affect trading.

The LLM never sends orders directly. It can adjust the approved strategy
parameters, while the role-specific fast loop and exchange rules remain in
control. If an API request fails or an invalid response is returned, the agent
keeps its previous valid strategy and the failure is recorded.

## Shocks and information

AML-Sim distinguishes between shocks by scope and by how much advance knowledge
participants receive.

### Micro shocks

Micro shocks are local to a company, instrument, or venue. Examples include
firm-specific negative news, recovery news, and unexpected order flow. They can
change fundamental value, sentiment, order arrival, volatility, or local
liquidity.

### Macro shocks

Macro shocks affect the wider financial system. Examples include interest-rate
changes, funding stress, broad liquidity stress, and changes in market-wide risk
aversion. Their effects may differ by asset class.

### Scheduled and unexpected events

An event may be announced before it becomes active, like a policy meeting or an
earnings release. Participants can then adjust expectations before the outcome.
Other events arrive without warning and test immediate resilience.

The system records:

- **Known events:** information the participant has received, including
  announcements and previous events.
- **Active events:** events currently applying direct effects to the participant
  or its market.

Information can also travel through a declared market relationship without
directly applying the original shock to the second market. This makes it
possible to separate information transmission from direct economic exposure.

## Using the dashboard

### 1. Start the local application

RabbitMQ must already be running. From the AML-Sim folder:

```bash
source .venv/bin/activate
python dashboard_server.py --port 8766
```

Open `http://127.0.0.1:8766` in a browser. Keep the terminal open while using
the dashboard and press `Ctrl+C` to stop it.

### 2. Choose a scenario

The scenario selector contains the available market configurations. A scenario
defines the instruments, exchanges, participants, shocks, relationships, and
simulation duration.

Research labels such as C0, D0, I1, A1, and C2 describe experimental
treatments, not automatically whether agents use an LLM. Check the participant
label or scenario description to confirm whether the slow strategist is Frozen
or OpenAI.

### 3. Choose a run ID

The run ID identifies one simulation and its reports. The dashboard suggests a
timestamped ID automatically. It can be replaced with a memorable ID containing
letters, numbers, underscores, or hyphens.

An existing ID is never overwritten. If the name already exists, the server
adds a numeric suffix.

### 4. Add participants if needed

Agent Studio allows users to add supported participant roles to the selected
scenario. For each group, the user can choose:

- participant name and role;
- instrument;
- number of instances;
- Frozen or OpenAI strategist;
- starting capital and risk mode;
- role-specific settings such as spread, quote size, trade probability,
  herding, alpha strategy, or shock sensitivity.

The server validates every value before launch. Agent Studio composes existing
AML roles; it does not execute uploaded or arbitrary Python code.

### 5. Run and monitor the simulation

Select **Run simulation**. During the run, the dashboard updates:

- market prices and returns;
- price paths for each instrument;
- best bid, best ask, spread, and order-book depth;
- completed trades and volume;
- active and observed shocks;
- participant activity, strategy type, position, and PnL;
- LLM completion, failure, and rejection status.

Simulations are finite. Their start time, end time, and tick interval come from
the selected scenario. A simulated hour does not necessarily take one real
hour. With the current clock settings, one simulated hour at 30-second ticks
usually takes around ten wall-clock minutes, plus model and reporting time.

### 6. Review completed runs

Completed runs remain available in the archive. Loading a run restores its
market paths, shocks, participant outcomes, and generated reports without
starting the simulation again.

## Reading the dashboard

### Markets

Shows every configured instrument, its latest simulated price, and return over
the run. Selecting a market changes the chart and order book shown in the main
workspace.

### Price chart

Displays the generated trade path. Shock markers show when events became active.
A visible price change near a marker is an observation, not automatically proof
that the shock caused the entire move; treatment comparisons and repeated runs
are needed for causal conclusions.

### Order book

Shows current resting asks above the market and bids below it, with size and
queue count at each level. The best ask is the lowest ask, and the best bid is
the highest bid.

### Participants

Shows configured roles and whether their slow strategist is Frozen or LLM. Once
reports are available, participant performance and activity can be compared.

### Shocks

Shows event type, scope, severity, timing, and affected markets. Scheduled
announcements, active events, expired events, and information-only delivery
should be interpreted separately.

### Reports

Reports provide the detailed record behind the dashboard, including:

- trade counts, volume, price paths, and market returns;
- order submissions, fills, and rejected orders;
- cash, positions, exposure, portfolio value, and PnL by participant;
- slow-loop strategy changes and LLM failure status;
- shock delivery and market-state changes;
- cross-market observations, decisions, and hedge outcomes;
- scenario, treatment, relationship, and random-seed metadata.

## A typical experiment

Suppose a user wants to study whether a stock-specific shock reaches its futures
market.

1. Run a disconnected baseline with the same participants and seed.
2. Run an information-only treatment where futures participants hear the news
   but no cross-market arbitrageur trades.
3. Run a connected treatment with information, valuation, and arbitrage links.
4. Repeat each treatment across several paired seeds.
5. Compare price response, lag, volume, liquidity, spreads, drawdowns, agent
   behavior, and hedge outcomes.

Frozen runs isolate the market mechanism. OpenAI runs can then test whether
adaptive decision-making changes the transmission or recovery pattern.

## Reading results carefully

- Compare treatments with the same seed and participant composition.
- Use several runs before drawing conclusions from averages or extreme events.
- Separate direct shock effects from information and trading transmission.
- Check whether every market remained liquid and continued receiving prices.
- Treat partial cross-market fills as residual risk, not successful arbitrage.
- Reconcile portfolio value, positions, and last prices before comparing PnL.
- Check LLM completion reports before describing a run as adaptive.
- Distinguish realized losses from mark-to-market changes in open inventory.

## Current limitations

AML-Sim is an active research platform rather than a production exchange or
investment product.

- Synthetic behavior depends on scenario design and calibration.
- Thin markets may produce feedback loops or unrealistic price movements.
- Cross-market order legs are not atomic and can fill unevenly.
- LLM behavior is bounded, but it can still reduce liquidity or make unstable
  parameter updates in poorly calibrated scenarios.
- Option Greeks, ETF creation/redemption, bond duration, margin, and settlement
  require further specialist implementations.
- The dashboard is intended for trusted local use and has no public-user
  authentication.
- Simulation results are experimental evidence, not financial advice, live
  prices, forecasts, or trading recommendations.

## Summary

AML-Sim provides a controlled environment for observing how heterogeneous and
adaptive market participants interact through realistic order books. Users can
build scenarios, inject uncertainty, connect markets, compare deterministic and
LLM-assisted behavior, monitor the simulation live, and inspect a complete
decision and outcome record afterward. Its central value is the ability to ask
"what changes if we intervene here?" and answer that question with repeatable
synthetic experiments.
